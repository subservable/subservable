# O-0021 — chaos-probe-as-interface-discovery

Status:
Observed

Context:
During Layer identity investigation, repeated interaction patterns revealed
that Subservable workflows naturally produce questions before implementation.

Observation:

The system appears to benefit from treating uncertainty as a probe target.

A probe does not provide an answer.
A probe exposes structure by creating a controlled disturbance.

Possible CLI implication:

The CLI should not primarily expose commands.
It should expose the next uncertainty reduction step.

Observed workflow:

Work
→ Observe
→ Capture artifact
→ Commit
→ Stop
→ Reflect

The CLI may eventually support this cadence by helping users answer:

- What changed?
- What claim is being tested?
- What evidence exists?
- What remains unknown?
- What is the next smallest experiment?

Potential identity:

Subservable may have multiple interaction drivers:

Researcher:
"I have a hypothesis. Help me design the next witness."

Builder:
"I have code. Help me preserve the decision history."

Explorer:
"I have data. Show me possible interpretations."

Reviewer:
"I need to understand why this exists."

Operator:
"I need deterministic execution."

Unknown:

Whether these represent separate CLI modes, personas, workflows,
or projections over the same underlying model.

Related observation:

Layer identity investigation revealed a similar pattern:

Expected:
Equivalent structures produce equivalent identities.

Observed:
Equivalent structures produced different identities.

Result:
The failure exposed an unclassified category.

The CLI may require the same approach:
failures and uncertainty are not errors to hide.
They are probes revealing missing distinctions.

Possible future model:

Subservable CLI may be less like a command executor
and more like a guided uncertainty-reduction instrument.

Unknown:

The final CLI shape.
