## Procedure vs. Context — working criterion

- **Procedure**: identified *and* reproducible on demand (intentional actions; also anything environmental/incidental once identified and controllable for).
- **Context**: shapes observation but is not reproducible on demand, even once identified (a passing gust, a sneeze, a random passerby).
- **"Unknown" is not a stable third category** — it's a transitional state between the two, resolved by investigation. A hidden influence (scratched lens coating, particulates) stays Context only until it's identified as reproducible; at that point it graduates to Procedure.
- **Open question, not settled**: reproducibility may not be the actual discriminator between Procedure and Context. Flagged on review but not yet resolved — held open rather than answered prematurely.

## Results

**R-1 — rock-observer / rock-creator symbol: resolved.**

Not a pair (two distinct roles sharing a body) and not a transform to something categorically unknowable (arrow/lambda to a "creator" or "God" primitive). Resolved instead as an **expand/collapse pair**: `rock ↔ rockstar` (equivalently "super rock" / "advanced rock").

- Same kind, more accumulated observation — not a different order of being.
- Reversible, not a one-way ontological jump. A creator that can't collapse back down would be a permanently other primitive; that's not what's being modeled.
- Satisfies the system's own constraint against overclaiming Discovery: asserting a categorically separate "creator" would smuggle in an unearned claim about the instructor's nature. `rockstar` requires no new substance — just more history on the same primitive.
- Ties back to "rock has time": the instructor isn't alien, it's closer to the rock's own future state, reached by accumulation rather than by crossing into something else.

Status: first resolved item. Provenance: closes the open naming question raised across Walk 2 and the follow-up exchange; supersedes the earlier `⟨rock, instructor⟩` pair framing and the lambda/lens symbol candidates.

## Next Procedures

### Counterexample Walk — best-effort replay protocol

1. **Best-effort replay of the route** (not "same route"). Openly noted: memory of the exact path isn't perfectly faithful — approximated, not guaranteed identical. That imprecision is recorded as part of provenance, not hidden.
2. **No prism.** No deliberate reorientation, no stopping-to-experiment, no jaywalking-as-experiment.
3. **Recording, in the moment**: bring laptop. Stop and jot a brief headline when something happens — no elaboration mid-walk, headline only, minimal interruption to the walk itself.
4. **No retrospective reconstruction.** If something wasn't written during the walk, it doesn't get filled in afterward. If a headline is missing or a stretch is a blank, record "forgot" as data rather than reconstructing what probably happened.
5. **After the walk**, sort what came up into: candidate Procedure (would recur if redone on purpose), Context (shaped observation but not summonable on demand), or unsorted (genuinely undetermined — leave open, don't force it).
6. **The test**: compare against Walk 2 at the level of structure — did anything Comparison/Witness/companion-projection-shaped show up without a prism to manufacture it? Deliberately not hunting for a "rainbow-companion-like" moment — that would be manipulating the observation again.

Purpose: test whether "intentional procedure" is carrying real explanatory weight, or whether comparable structure emerges regardless — which would suggest some of what Walk 2 attributed to Procedure was actually ambient Context that the prism may have made legible rather than caused.

### Alternative: Redux Walk

Different question than the Counterexample Walk — not "what happens with no procedure" but "is Walk 2 reproducible at all." Repeat Walk 2 as faithfully as possible: same route (best-effort), prism, same intent to reorient/turn/pause and inspect the companion projection.

- If the redux produces a *similar* structure to Walk 2 → the prism-procedure is doing reproducible work; Walk 2 wasn't a one-off.
- If the redux differs substantially from Walk 2 → intentional procedure alone doesn't guarantee the same observations, which would complicate the Counterexample Walk's interpretation too (a null result there wouldn't cleanly mean "procedure doesn't matter" if procedure itself isn't reproducible).

Doing both, in either order, gives two independent comparison points against Walk 2 rather than one — an application of the minimum-witness-of-three intuition to the method itself.
