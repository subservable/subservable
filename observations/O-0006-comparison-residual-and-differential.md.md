# O-0006-comparison-residual-and-differential

## Statement

Comparison between expected and observed structures may produce a
measurable remainder.

This remainder appears in multiple exploratory forms:

- differential between competing claims
- residual between an expected model and an observed signal

These forms may share a common underlying relationship, but no collapse
of these concepts is currently established.

## Context

During exploration of claim comparison and signal evaluation, a recurring
shape appeared:

    expected
        |
        v
    comparison
        |
        v
    remaining difference

This shape is suggestive but insufficient to define a shared primitive.

The current observation is not that all differences are equivalent.

The current observation is that the remainder produced by comparison may
itself be valuable data.

## Symbol Discipline

The symbol Δ remains reserved for the Foundation transform.

Claim differentials and signal residuals represent relationships between
entities, not transformation of a single entity.

No alternate mathematical notation is introduced here.

## Examples

### Claim Differential

A comparison between competing claims may reveal:

- where claims agree
- where claims diverge
- what assumptions differ
- what evidence contributes to the difference

Example:

rock-age hypothesis vs productive morphology explanation.

The difference itself may become analyzable data.

### Signal Residual

A comparison between an expected model and an observed signal may reveal:

- unexplained remainder
- deviation from prediction
- potential missing structure

No concrete residual experiment has yet been performed.

## Witnesses

- Rock-age morphology contest
  - demonstrates a claim differential
- Future signal comparison experiments
  - required before testing whether residual and differential share a
    common structure

## Status

-exploratory

## Constraints

No new primitive defined.

No symbol introduced.

No claim that all comparison relationships are identical.

This observation only preserves a possible relationship for future testing.

## Questions

- Are claim differentials and signal residuals instances of the same
  comparison structure?
- What test would distinguish them if they are different?
- Is there a case where one exists without the other?
- Does preserving the remainder of a comparison create useful future
  information?

## Required Test

A future test must compare at least:

1. one real claim differential
2. one real signal residual

The result should determine whether:

- they collapse into one reusable comparison primitive
- they remain separate concepts
- the apparent similarity was only structural analogy
