# O-0005-maw-preservation-and-retrieval-tension

## Statement

Preservation (the MAW instinct — store everything, interpret nothing,
discard nothing) is not the same problem as retrieval (how preserved
material later becomes available for examination).

The MAW addresses preservation. It does not address retrieval.

## Context

Noticed while considering why "MAW" felt like the right instinct: the
desire is to store first and decide what matters never, later, or on
demand — not to avoid interpretation, but to avoid destroying information
before it is known whether interpretation will ever be needed.

The open problem is not whether to preserve.

The open problem is how preserved material becomes available for reasoning.

This distinction may represent a recurring separation between storage of
possibility and access to possibility.

## Witnesses

- Claim lifecycle bootstrap (O-0000) — demonstrates a preservation and
  replay path without requiring interpretation, though not yet retrieval
  or indexing
- MAW/Bedrock vocabulary observations (O-0002)
- Multi-lens selection discussion
- Bluetooth/Arduino exploration about accessing preserved contents

## Constraints

No indexing, retrieval, or access architecture proposed.

This does not modify the MAW/preservation model.

This observation only names the distinction between preservation and
retrieval as separate problems.
