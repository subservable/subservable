# Prism Model v0.1

## Purpose

A Prism defines a coherent perspective through which immutable observations may be examined.

A Prism does not alter observations.

A Prism defines the procedures, assumptions, and interpretation rules used to derive representations.

---

# Definition

A Prism is a coherent collection of procedures:

\[
Prism \subseteq \mathcal{F}
\]

where:

\[
\mathcal{F}
\]

is the family of deterministic procedures.

A Prism includes:

- procedures,
- assumptions,
- domain context,
- interpretation rules.

---

# Purpose of a Prism

The same observations MAY produce different representations under different Prisms.

Given:

\[
M
\]

two Prisms:

\[
P_1
\]

and:

\[
P_2
\]

may produce:

\[
A_1=P_1(M)
\]

and:

\[
A_2=P_2(M)
\]

Neither representation modifies the underlying observations.

---

# Examples

Examples of Prisms include:

## Scientific Prism

May emphasize:

- measurement,
- causal relationships,
- reproducibility,
- statistical inference.

## Economic Prism

May emphasize:

- incentives,
- resources,
- exchanges,
- optimization.

## Biological Prism

May emphasize:

- adaptation,
- systems,
- evolution,
- interaction.

## Linguistic Prism

May emphasize:

- symbols,
- relationships,
- meaning,
- communication.

---

# Prism Assumptions

A Prism SHOULD explicitly define assumptions that influence interpretation.

Examples:

- mathematical assumptions,
- domain assumptions,
- model constraints,
- accepted uncertainty models.

Assumptions MUST be represented explicitly where they influence procedure outputs.

---

# Prism and Truth

A Prism does not define truth.

A Prism defines a method of examination.

Different Prisms MAY reveal different structures from identical observations.

The observations remain immutable.

Only the representation changes.

---

# Relationship to Spectra

A Spectrum is a representation produced by applying procedures within a Prism.

\[
\Omega=T_R(R)
\]

The Prism provides the lens.

The Spectrum is the resulting representation.
