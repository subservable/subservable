# Discovery Process

## Purpose

This document preserves the replayable process through which this claim evolved.

It records exploration.

It does not establish proof.

---

## Observation

Rocks visibly preserve accumulated layers.

Natural language suggested a possible relationship through the expression:

rock + age

This observation motivated exploration.

---

## Initial Interpretation

The initial hypothesis proposed that accumulated relationships might naturally emerge through semantic suffixes.

---

## Challenge

Independent evaluation compared the observation against productive English morphology.

Known productive patterns include:

- break → breakage
- drain → drainage
- mile → mileage
- dose → dosage

"Rock" belongs to neither productive family.

The apparent relationship arises from the juxtaposition of the free words "rock" and "age" rather than productive suffixation.

---

## Refinement

The original suffix hypothesis was not supported.

The exploration instead clarified a more fundamental distinction:

Semantic relationships may emerge through replayable evaluation.

Specific language constructions must themselves be independently evaluated rather than assumed.

---

## Current Understanding

The valuable outcome was not a newly discovered suffix.

The valuable outcome was a replayable process capable of refining an attractive intuition into a narrower, more reproducible claim.
