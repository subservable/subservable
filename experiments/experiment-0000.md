# Experiment 0000

## Title

Parallel Narrative Edge Detection

## Purpose

Evaluate whether multiple independent retellings naturally reveal stable structural edges through preservation and projection.

This experiment is not intended to determine historical truth, identify a canonical narrative, or reconcile differing accounts.

Its purpose is to evaluate whether Subservable can preserve independent observations and later expose where those observations naturally converge and diverge.

---

## Motivation

Subservable preserves observations before interpretation.

If several independent representations describe overlapping events, the system should preserve each representation independently.

Only after preservation should projections be allowed to reveal shared structure and divergence.

The experiment asks whether meaningful structure can emerge without requiring semantic reconciliation.

---

## Initial Dataset

Public-domain texts:

- Matthew
- Luke
- John

These texts are selected because they describe overlapping narratives while remaining independently preserved.

The dataset is intentionally small.

The objective is to evaluate the projection mechanism rather than statistical confidence.

---

## Primary Design Question

### What is the atomic Observation?

This experiment intentionally leaves the Observation boundary undefined.

Candidate boundaries include:

- document
- chapter
- section
- event
- verse

Each boundary represents a distinct experiment.

Selecting an Observation boundary is therefore the first replayable step.

---

## Procedure

1. Select an Observation boundary.
2. Import each narrative independently.
3. Preserve every Observation without interpretation.
4. Record deterministic provenance.
5. Replay reconstruction.
6. Generate one or more projections.
7. Define the projection rule.
8. Define the criteria for identifying:

   - convergence,
   - divergence,
   - unrelated observations.

No reconciliation is performed.

---

## Questions

- Does a deterministic projection rule produce replayable results?
- Which projection rules produce useful structure?
- Which projection rules overfit unrelated observations?
- Which projection rules preserve divergence without forcing reconciliation?

---

## Expected Outputs

The experiment may produce:

- Observations
- Supporting assets
- Provenance records
- Artifacts
- Timeline projections
- Graph projections
- Future claims

The experiment may also demonstrate that a chosen Observation boundary is unsuitable.

That remains a successful experimental outcome if it is replayable.

---

## Constraints

This experiment does not attempt to:

- determine historical truth,
- reconcile narratives,
- infer author intent,
- infer audience,
- collapse differing observations,
- modify preserved observations.

Differences are preserved.

---

## Success Criteria

The experiment is successful if:

- observations remain independently reconstructable,
- provenance remains deterministic,
- projections expose both convergence and divergence,
- preserved observations remain unchanged.

The experiment does **not** require agreement between the source narratives.
