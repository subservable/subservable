# Guerrilla Experiment — Physical Subservable

**Status:** Draft  
**Purpose:** Test whether the fundamental properties of Subservable can survive without computational infrastructure.

## Premise

Subservable is intended to preserve observations independently of the mechanisms later used to interpret them.

This experiment removes those mechanisms.

Assume:

- electricity is unavailable;
- computers are unavailable;
- networks are unavailable;
- software is unavailable;
- the collector may have no reliable access to anything except physical materials.

The question becomes:

> What is the smallest physical system capable of preserving an observation and its sequence so that interpretation can occur later?

---

## Procedure

A physical journal is used as the recording medium.

Material is placed into the journal according to the order in which it is collected.

The journal therefore provides two distinct properties:

\[
A = (x,\Delta,R)
\]

where:

- \(x\) = surviving artifact;
- \(\Delta\) = decomposition into representations;
- \(R\) = reconstruction procedure.

For this experiment, the physical journal additionally provides an ordering relation:

\[
x_1 \prec x_2 \prec x_3 \ldots
\]

where the ordering is determined by the physical sequence of pages.

The journal is therefore not merely storage.

It preserves **artifact + sequence**.

---

## Deliberate Separation

The collector's interpretation is intentionally excluded from the captured material.

The experiment attempts to preserve:

1. **what survived collection;**
2. **where it occurs in sequence;**
3. **the physical relationship between the artifact and its recording medium.**

It does not attempt to preserve:

- why the artifact was collected;
- what the collector believed it meant;
- what pattern the collector expected;
- what hypothesis the collector was testing;
- what interpretation may eventually be assigned to it.

Those belong to a later layer.

---

## Raw Field Kit

The physical journal functions as a minimal field kit for Subservable.

A field entry may contain:

\[
E_n = (x_n, p_n)
\]

where:

- \(x_n\) = collected artifact;
- \(p_n\) = physical position in the sequence.

The position is not merely metadata added afterward.

The page itself participates in preserving the history.

---

## Interpretation Boundary

The experiment establishes an intentional boundary:

\[
\boxed{\text{Collected Artifact + Sequence}}
\]

is preserved independently from:

\[
\boxed{\text{Collector Interpretation}}
\]

The first is the experimental record.

The second is a subsequent operation upon that record.

This distinction is important because interpretation may change while the physical record remains unchanged.

---

## Playback

The journal can later be treated as a playback medium.

A later observer may encounter:

\[
E_1 \rightarrow E_2 \rightarrow E_3 \rightarrow \ldots \rightarrow E_n
\]

without initially knowing why those artifacts were collected.

The observer can therefore attempt reconstruction or interpretation from the surviving record rather than from the collector's explanation.

This provides a deliberately crude physical analogue of:

\[
\text{Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Sequence}
\rightarrow
\text{Playback}
\rightarrow
\text{Interpretation}
\]

---

## What This Experiment Tests

The experiment is not initially testing whether the artifacts contain meaningful patterns.

It is testing something more primitive:

> Can a record preserve enough of its history to remain observable after the machinery that created and interpreted it is removed?

If yes, then the physical record becomes a minimal witness for the distinction between:

- observation and interpretation;
- artifact and representation;
- history and explanation;
- collection and discovery.

---

## First Test

The first test consists of providing the surviving artifact sequence to an observer without providing the collector's hidden interpretation.

The observer may:

- inspect the artifacts;
- inspect their physical order;
- reconstruct the sequence;
- describe observable relationships;
- propose interpretations.

The observer should not be told the collector's interpretation before the initial observation.

This allows the experiment to distinguish:

\[
\text{What was collected}
\]

from:

\[
\text{What the collector thought it meant}.
\]

---

## Experimental Discipline

For the initial playback, interpretation should remain downstream.

The record should be allowed to speak before the collector explains it.

No conclusion is required.

A successful experiment may consist only of demonstrating that:

\[
\boxed{
\text{artifact + preserved sequence}
}
\]

can survive independently of:

\[
\boxed{
\text{interpreter + infrastructure}
}
\]
